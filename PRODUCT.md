# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Personal tool built by and for the developer: a Ukrainian adult learning English at depth. Context of use: at a desk, focused, wanting to review vocabulary quickly and get out. Not a passive learner; expects the app to stay out of the way and let the content do the work.

## Product Purpose

A vocabulary study tool for Ukrainian speakers learning English. Core loop: build decks of word-definition pairs, study them through multiple modes (flip cards, quiz), look up words in an integrated dictionary, and track progress over time. Feature parity goal: Quizlet's structure + Cambridge Dictionary's depth, without either product's visual noise.

## Brand Personality

Sharp, deliberate, unhurried. The app knows what it is. It does not congratulate you for showing up; it just works. Closest reference: Raycast (command-palette feel, dark-native, every pixel earns its place).

## Anti-references

- **Duolingo**: No streaks, no mascots, no confetti, no gamified reward loops. Learning is the reward.
- **Generic SaaS**: No gradient hero cards, no "10,000 words learned" stat banners, no identical icon-grid layouts.

## Design Principles

1. **Get to the card fast.** Every interaction should minimize steps between the user and studying. Navigation is infrastructure, not the product.
2. **Dark is the identity, not a mode.** The dark theme is chosen, not offered as a toggle. It should feel native and intentional, not just dark-mode CSS.
3. **Keyboard-first.** Power paths are always reachable without a mouse (Cmd/Ctrl+K for lookup is the model; extend that philosophy everywhere).
4. **Restraint in feedback.** Correct/incorrect signals should be precise (color, icon, sound if any) — not animated celebrations. The content deserves more space than the UI's reactions to it.
5. **Content is the hierarchy.** Word text, definitions, and example sentences should dominate visual weight. UI chrome (borders, labels, icons) should recede.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Good contrast and keyboard navigation as a baseline. No specific WCAG level mandated, but text should be legible and interactive elements focusable. Reduced-motion not required but keep animations purposeful and short.
